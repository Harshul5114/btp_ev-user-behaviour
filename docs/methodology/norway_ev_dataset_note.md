# Norway residential EV charging data: source and use note

Source: Sørensen et al., *Electric vehicle charging dataset with 35,000 charging sessions from 12 residential locations in Norway*, Data in Brief 57 (2024), 110883, DOI [10.1016/j.dib.2024.110883](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2024.110883). The supplied copy is `data/raw/norway_ev/docs/1-s2.0-S2352340924008461-main.pdf`; the four raw CSVs are in `data/raw/norway_ev/`. The public files are semicolon-delimited, with comma decimal separators. The notebook `notebooks/04_ev_charging_data_analysis.ipynb` reads them without changing them.

## Scope and provenance

The data are charging reports from **12 residential or shared residential locations in Norway**, collected from charge-point operators and housing cooperatives. The reports span different periods across locations during 2018–2021. They do **not** directly characterize workplace or public charging. The 267 `user_id` values are repeat charging identifiers, not a representative sample of all Norwegian EV owners. The paper says time fields use local CET with daylight saving time; the notebook preserves the supplied local clock times and uses the supplied connection duration rather than inferring timezone-aware elapsed time.

| Dataset | Row and key | Source-backed field meanings and provenance |
|---|---|---|
| `Dataset1_charging_reports.csv` | One charging report per unique `session_id`; `user_id` links a recurring user, `location` a residential site. | **Original charging report:** `location`, `user_id`, `session_id`, `plugin_time`, `plugout_time`, `energy_session` (energy charged, kWh). **Calculated within the original report:** `connection_time` (hours from the connection interval). Original report data are not battery telemetry; `energy_session` is delivered charging energy, not directly measured energy stored in the battery. |
| `Dataset2_user_predictions.csv` | One row per `user_id`. | **Predicted:** `charging_power` (kW), estimated from high observed session-average power under the paper's unplug-while-charging assumption; `battery_capacity` (net battery capacity, kWh), inferred from maximum delivered session energy, an 88% efficiency assumption and assumed SoC ranges. Table 2 prints `kW` for capacity, but its energy/range equation has units of **kWh**. Six users lack both predictions. |
| `Dataset3_session_predictions.csv` | One row per predicted `session_id`, linked to Dataset 1 by `session_id` and checked against `user_id`; only users with Dataset 2 predictions. | **Calculated from measured inputs plus predicted power:** `charging_time` and `idle_time` (hours). **Predicted/assumption-based:** `SoC_diff` and `SoC_start` (%), with an assumed 95% end SoC; `idle_session` (kWh of potential idle energy capacity). `non_flex_session` contains observed `energy_session` only for sessions classified with predicted idle time below 1 hour; its blank values are mostly not-applicable. |
| `Dataset4_hourly_predictions.csv` | One generated hour segment per `session_id` and `date_from`; repeated session IDs. | `date_from` is the hour start. `energy_charged_i`, `energy_idle_i`, `energy_connected_i` (kWh per hour) and `SoC_diff_i`, `SoC_from_i`, `SoC_to_i` (%) are **model outputs**, based on predicted user power, immediate charging after plug-in, constant charging power, predicted capacity, and the SoC assumptions. `energy_connected_i` is potential connected capacity, not measured consumption. |

The paper describes its original report cleaning before release, including limits on short/long connections and unusually high average power. The notebook does not repeat or extend that cleaning. It audits missingness and inconsistencies, then uses all records supported by each statistic. Dataset 1 contains 35,377 sessions; 38 have no plug-out or connection duration. It also contains 439 reports at or below 0.5 kWh despite the paper's listed low-energy cleaning threshold. The files do not establish why these remain, so the notebook retains and flags them. For 26 complete sessions, the supplied duration differs from the difference of the naive local timestamp strings by more than five minutes (maximum two hours); the notebook uses supplied `connection_time` and does not silently reconcile the discrepancy. Dataset 3 covers 34,537 sessions (261 users); its 179 missing `charging_time`, `idle_time`, and `idle_session` values follow the paper's rule for cases where inferred charging time would exceed connection time. Dataset 4 has 6,710 rows with no user or session ID and zero `energy_connected_i`; these cannot be joined to sessions and are kept out of session-level summaries.

## Appropriate use in the next project stage

The strongest empirical checks for an eventual NHTS-to-EV model are the **residential** plug-in/plug-out clock distributions, delivered energy per session, and connection duration, plus the range of per-user recorded session energy. These describe charging at sampled residential charge points, not the probability of charging on every home arrival. The notebook also calculates delivered energy divided by whole connection duration as an observed-input average rate; it includes idle time and is not instantaneous active charging power. Raw per-user session counts can be reported, but a comparable charging rate per user needs a reliable observation window for each user and their charging elsewhere. No survey weights are supplied; figure percentages describe recorded sessions, not a national EV population.

Predicted `charging_power`, `battery_capacity`, `charging_time`, `idle_time`, hourly energy profiles, and all SoC values should be used only for scenario checks or sensitivity analysis. They are not independent measurements of EV hardware, instantaneous charging power, battery SoC, or actual unplugged charging flexibility. In particular, the difference between connection and predicted active charging time is a **model-dependent** idle estimate.
